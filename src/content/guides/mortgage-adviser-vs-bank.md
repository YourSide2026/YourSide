When it’s time to get a home loan, most people face the same question: *should I go straight to my bank, or use a mortgage adviser?* Both can get you a mortgage, but the experience, and sometimes the outcome, can be quite different. Here’s an honest comparison to help you decide.

## The key difference

A **bank** employs its own lending staff, who can only offer that bank’s products. Their job is to lend you the bank’s money on the bank’s terms.

A **mortgage adviser** works with a range of lenders. Their job is to find the option that best fits you, and New Zealand law requires them to put your interests first when they give advice.

## Mortgage adviser vs bank at a glance

| | Going direct to a bank | Using a mortgage adviser |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Choice** | One lender’s products | Options from multiple lenders |
| **Cost to you** | Usually free | Usually free (the lender pays the adviser) |
| **Who they work for** | The bank | You, with a legal duty to put your interests first |
| **Rate negotiation** | You negotiate yourself | The adviser negotiates on your behalf |
| **Paperwork** | You manage each application | The adviser manages it for you |
| **If you’re declined** | Start again with another bank | The adviser takes your application to a lender that fits |
| **After settlement** | Depends on the bank | Ongoing reviews when rates end or life changes |

## Choice: one lender vs many

Every lender has its own policies. These cover how it treats self-employed income, bonuses and overtime, how much it will lend against a new build, how it views a small deposit, and much more. A bank can only tell you what *it* will do.

An adviser knows how a range of lenders operate and what each is looking for. That can mean the difference between a “no” and a “yes”, or between a good deal and a great one.

## Cost: is a mortgage adviser more expensive?

For most people, no. Advisers are generally paid a commission by the lender when your loan settles, and lenders don’t charge you a higher rate for using one.

Advisers must tell you upfront how they’re paid and whether any fees could apply. A fee might apply, for example, if you refinance again soon after settlement. Read more about [how mortgage advisers are paid](/guides/what-is-a-mortgage-adviser/).

## Interest rates: who gets the better deal?

Banks advertise their rates publicly, and you can negotiate yourself. But advisers deal with lenders every day. They know what’s on offer, what discounts are available and how to present your application so you’re seen as a strong borrower. They can also compare cash contributions and fees across lenders, not just the headline rate.

The best rate on paper isn’t always the best deal overall. Loan structure, flexibility and fees all matter. See today’s numbers on our [interest rates page](/interest-rates/).

## Time and paperwork

If you want to compare three banks yourself, you’ll be filling in three applications, gathering documents three times and answering three sets of questions. An adviser collects your information once, works out which lenders suit you best, and handles the back-and-forth for you.

## When going direct to your bank makes sense

To be fair, using an adviser isn’t the only way. Going direct can work well if:

- Your situation is simple, for example salaried income, a healthy deposit and no complicated debts
- You have a long, strong relationship with your bank and are happy with its offer
- You’re confident comparing lenders and negotiating yourself
- You specifically want a lender that your adviser doesn’t work with

## When a mortgage adviser adds the most value

An adviser is especially worth talking to if you:

- Are a **first home buyer** and want someone to explain each step
- Are **self-employed** or have irregular income
- Have a **smaller deposit**
- Are **buying an investment property** or restructuring a portfolio
- Want to **refinance** and see what other lenders would offer
- Have been **declined** by a bank before
- Simply want an expert in your corner and less stress

## So, which is better?

For most home buyers, a mortgage adviser offers more choice, more support and usually no extra cost. The bank is still where your loan ends up, but an adviser helps make sure it’s the *right* bank and the *right* structure for you.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use an adviser if I already have pre-approval from my bank?

Yes. An adviser can compare your bank’s offer against other lenders and tell you whether it’s competitive.

### Will an adviser recommend my current bank?

If it’s the best fit for you, absolutely. Advisers aren’t tied to any one lender, so if your bank is the best fit, that’s what they’ll recommend.

### Do mortgage advisers work with every lender?

Not always. Each adviser works with a panel of lenders. Ask which lenders they use. A good adviser will be upfront about it.
