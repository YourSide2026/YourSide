If you’ve started looking into home loans, you’ve probably heard people recommend “just talking to a mortgage adviser.” But what does a mortgage adviser actually do, how are they paid, and how do you know you can trust them? Here’s what you need to know.

## What is a mortgage adviser?

A mortgage adviser, often called a **mortgage broker**, is a qualified financial adviser who specialises in home lending. Instead of working for one bank, they work with a range of lenders. Their job is to understand your situation, find the lending options that suit you and guide you through the process of getting your loan approved.

Think of them as an expert in your corner. You only deal with one person, and they deal with the lenders.

## What does a mortgage adviser do?

The details vary depending on your situation, but a good adviser will:

1. **Get to know you.** They’ll ask about your income, spending, savings, goals and plans so their advice fits your life, not just today’s numbers.
2. **Work out what you can borrow.** They’ll check how different lenders would assess you, which can vary more than you might expect.
3. **Compare lenders and loan options.** That includes interest rates, any cash contributions, fees, policies and flexible features.
4. **Prepare and submit your application.** They’ll present your application in the best possible light, which is especially valuable if your situation is less straightforward.
5. **Negotiate on your behalf.** Advisers know what lenders are offering and can often negotiate on rates or terms.
6. **Structure your loan.** They’ll help you decide how to split your loan between fixed and floating rates, and over what terms, so it suits your plans.
7. **Guide you to settlement.** They’ll work with your lawyer and the lender to make sure everything is in place on the day.
8. **Stay in touch afterwards.** A good adviser checks in when your fixed rate is ending and when your circumstances change.

## How are mortgage advisers paid?

Most mortgage advisers in New Zealand are paid a **commission by the lender** when your loan settles. That means most people don’t pay a fee for mortgage advice.

There are some situations where a fee may apply. For example, an adviser may charge a fee if you repay or refinance your loan shortly after settlement, because the lender may claw back their commission. Every adviser must explain how they’re paid, and any fees you might be charged, **before** they give you advice. If anything is unclear, ask.

## Are mortgage advisers regulated in New Zealand?

Yes. Mortgage advice is financial advice under New Zealand law, so advisers are regulated by the Financial Markets Authority (FMA). That means:

- Advice must be given by, or under, a **licensed financial advice provider**.
- Advisers must follow the **Code of Professional Conduct for Financial Advice Services**, which sets standards for competence, knowledge and skill.
- Advisers must **put your interests first** when giving advice, even though a lender pays them.
- Advisers must **disclose** how they’re paid, any conflicts of interest, and how to complain if something goes wrong.
- Providers must belong to an independent **dispute resolution scheme**, which offers free help if you can’t resolve a complaint.

You can check a provider is registered on the Financial Service Providers Register.

## Mortgage adviser vs mortgage broker: is there a difference?

In practice, no. “Mortgage broker” is the more traditional term. “Mortgage adviser” reflects the regulated, advice-based role advisers play today. Both describe someone who compares lenders and helps you get a home loan.

## Who should use a mortgage adviser?

Anyone with a mortgage can benefit, but advisers are especially helpful if you are:

- **A first home buyer** working through deposits, KiwiSaver and pre-approval for the first time
- **Self-employed**, or on variable or commission-based income
- **Buying with a smaller deposit**
- **Investing in property** or building a portfolio
- **Refinancing** to get a better deal or restructure your lending
- **Building, or buying off the plans**
- Someone who’s **been turned down** by a bank before

## How to choose a good mortgage adviser

Look for someone who:

- Takes the time to understand your goals, not just your income
- Explains things clearly and doesn’t rush you
- Is upfront about how they’re paid and which lenders they work with
- Has experience with situations like yours
- Is easy to get hold of throughout the process

Good questions to ask include: *How many lenders do you work with? How are you paid? Will I ever have to pay a fee? Will you stay in touch after settlement?*

## Frequently asked questions

### Does using a mortgage adviser affect my interest rate?

Advisers can often get rates that are as good as, or better than, what you’d be offered by going direct, because lenders compete for their clients’ business.

### Will using an adviser slow things down?

Usually the opposite. Advisers know what each lender needs, so applications tend to be complete the first time.

### Can a mortgage adviser help if my bank has said no?

Often, yes. Different lenders have different policies, and what doesn’t work for one may suit another.
